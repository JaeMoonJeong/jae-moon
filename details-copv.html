Development of optimal curing cycle for the filament wound pressure vessel

satisfies the minimization of pressure vessel defects, curing uniformity, and improvement of physical properties

Project Goal: Optimizing the Cure Process Window
Technical Approach: Multiscale Consolidation Modeling

Situation

  • Surging demand for composite pressure vessels: Ultralight, Non-explosive, Anti-Corrosive (Auto/Aero/Drones).
  • Inherent manufacturing constraints of winding tension limiting resin flow and causing defects.
    • Hoop section: Difficulty in void discharge caused by excessive compression from final winding layers.
    • Dome Section: High risk of delamination due to frequent material overlaps and complex geometry.

Task

  • Derivation of an optimized curing cycle to minimize internal voids and maximize structural reliability.
  • Transition from qualitative resin-only approaches to integrated multi-physics data analysis::
    • Data-driven modeling of Cure Kinetics, exothermic reactions, and viscosity transitions.
    • Characterization of thermal expansion, and tension-induced pressure distribution.

Action

  • Multi-scale Modeling Approach: Systematic optimization of the curing cycle through hierarchical simulation.
    • Micro-scale (Fiber Level): Fiber-level micro-space modeling for resin flow permeability indexing.
    • Meso-scale (Tow/Interface Level): Internal flow field analysis at interfaces using derived permeability.
    • Macro-scale (Full Structure): Optimal curing parameter establishment via multiphase void migration tracking.

Result

  • Defect Suppression: Successful reduction of internal void below 5%.
  • Model Validation: Simulation reliability within 20% error of experimental data.
  • Performance Maximization: Enhanced burst pressure and fatigue life through uniform curing (degree of cure deviation < 5%).
