Sustainable Energy System Optimization

Advancing high-efficiency energy storage system through structural synergy and advanced materials for VRFB

Study 1: Pseudo-channel CP Electrode
Study 2: Integrated BP-Electrode Structure
Study 3: Stiffness Gradient Carbon Felt
Study 4: Aligned CNT Bipolar Plate

Situation

  • Global energy paradigm shift toward carbon neutrality and high-efficiency hydrogen/redox systems.
  • Strategic necessity for core technical synergy between PEMFC and VRFB infrastructures.

Task

  • Minimization of interfacial contact resistance and optimization of mass transfer.
  • Development of high-performance components including Bipolar Plates and advanced Electrodes.

Action

  • End-to-end engineering: From individual part fabrication to full cell assembly and performance testing.
  • Study 1 (Electrode Morphology):
    Fabrication of holey-patterned carbon paper electrodes - **Achieved 10.41% increase in energy efficiency via pseudo-channel effects.**
  • Study 2 (Interfacial Integration):
    Design of contact-resistance-free integrated BP-electrode structures - **Achieved 3% higher system efficiency by eliminating mechanical interfaces.**
  • Study 3 (Stiffness Gradient):
    Development of stiffness-gradient electrodes - **Achieved 86.5% energy efficiency (10% increase) by optimizing local compression and porosity.**
  • Study 4 (Advanced Nanocomposite):
    Synthesis of horizontally aligned CNT nanocomposite bipolar plates - **Reduced potential drop by 40% and improved capacity by 14%.**

Result

  • System-Level Breakthrough: Verified superior performance through direct execution of cell assembly and electrochemical testing.
  • Proven Reliability: Achieved high-efficiency benchmarks (Max 86.5% EE) and significant ohmic loss reduction.

Project Information

  • Project Name: Light Weight HT-PEMFC and VRFB
  • Category: Energy Storage
  • Client: EEWS (Energy, Environment, Water, and Sustainability) at KAIST
  • Software Stack: COMSOL Multiphysics, SOLIDWORKS, ANSYS Fluent
